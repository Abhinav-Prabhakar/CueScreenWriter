EXT. THE LAMP – DAY, EVENING, NIGHT

A lamp that keeps your hours.

Cue’s light follows the time of day: clean paper in the morning, a warm glow in the evening, a dark desk after midnight. Or pick one and keep it. (This page does the same; try the switch.)

INT. THE TITLE PAGE – OPENING

Your title, the way you see it.

Give your script its own title graphic, like a film’s logo on its poster. It takes the place of the typed title on the title page, on your script’s cover in the library, and in the PDF you send out.

INT. THE DESK – AROUND THE PAGE

Pin up what you’re seeing.

Drag photos, maps or storyboards onto the desk beside your pages. They stay where you put them, saved with the script, and they never print.

INT. THE DESK – LATER

Room for the mess.

Sticky notes sit on the desk beside the script for the thoughts that don’t belong to one line. Tint them, cross things off, tidy them away. Notes on a line live in the margin.

INT. THE PAGE – AS YOU TYPE

Formatting that stays out of your way.

Press Enter and Cue offers what comes next: action, a character, a new scene. Type a name in capitals and it knows it’s a character. Type I and it offers INT. You never stop to pick a style from a menu.

INT. QUESTIONS – CONTINUOUS

Questions

Honest answers, in the order people ask them.

Something else? Open an issue.

Where can I get it?

Download the latest build from GitHub Releases, unzip it, and drag Cue to Applications. It’s free.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac running macOS 15 Sequoia or later.

Can I bring my scripts from Final Draft or another app?

Yes, as a PDF or a Fountain file. Cue reads the page and rebuilds the scenes, characters and dialogue. Final Draft’s own .fdx files aren’t supported yet, so export a PDF from Final Draft and import that.

Can I send my script to someone who doesn’t use Cue?

Yes. Cue exports a standard screenplay PDF that anyone can read, print or submit, and Fountain, which most screenwriting apps can open.

Is there an iPad version?

Not yet. The Mac comes first. An iPad version is planned.

Why is it free?

Because a place to write shouldn’t cost more than the coffee you drink while using it. Cue has no servers to run and no subscription to justify, and the source is open on GitHub. Download it, keep it, write.

Is Cue made with AI?

Partly, and we’d rather say so. Cue is designed by Daniel Millyard, a designer and writer, and built with Claude, an AI coding assistant. The app itself has no AI in it: it doesn’t write for you, read your script or send it anywhere. It only ever suggests words you’ve already written, like a character’s name.

What isn’t Cue?

Cue is for writing. It doesn’t do live collaboration, revision colours or production breakdowns. If your production needs those, draft in Cue and finish there.
